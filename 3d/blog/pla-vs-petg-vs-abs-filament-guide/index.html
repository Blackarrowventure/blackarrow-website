PLA vs PETG vs ABS: Which 3D Printing Filament Should You Use?

Picking the wrong filament for the job is the most common beginner mistake after bed leveling. Here's what each material is actually good for.

PLA — the default, and for good reason

PLA prints at lower temperatures, barely warps, and needs no enclosure or heated chamber to come out clean. It's the right choice for figurines, prototypes, gifts, and most things that won't sit in a hot car or take a hard knock. Our PLA Filament 1kg comes in 8 colors and works on every FDM printer we sell.

PETG — tougher, and handles moisture and UV better

PETG is a step up in durability and heat resistance over PLA, with better outdoor and moisture resistance, at the cost of being slightly trickier to print (more stringing if your settings aren't dialed in). It's a good choice for functional parts — brackets, outdoor mounts, containers.

ABS and engineering materials — when you need real heat resistance

ABS, ASA, PC and carbon/glass-fiber-reinforced filaments need higher nozzle and bed temperatures, and ideally an enclosed, heated chamber to avoid warping and layer separation. This is where hardware matters: the Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 heats its nozzle up to 350°C and its bed up to 110°C, and the Flashforge Creator 5 Pro adds a heated chamber up to 65°C with a 320°C hot end specifically to support ABS, ASA, PC and CF/GF materials.

MaterialBest forNeeds
PLAFigurines, prototypes, gifts, low-stress partsNo enclosure needed
PETGFunctional parts, outdoor use, moisture exposureSlightly higher nozzle temp
ABS / ASA / PCHeat-resistant, mechanically demanding partsHeated bed, ideally an enclosure

Nozzle replacement matters too

Abrasive materials (carbon-fiber, glass-fiber filament) wear down brass nozzles quickly. If you're printing engineering materials regularly, keep a spare hot end on hand — our Bambu Hotend — A1 Series is available in four nozzle sizes for exactly this.
