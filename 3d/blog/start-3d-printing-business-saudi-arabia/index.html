How to Start a Small 3D Printing Business in Saudi Arabia

A single desktop 3D printer can be the start of a real side business in Saudi Arabia. This guide covers what sells, what it costs to start, how to price each print, and where to sell.

Quick answer

You can start a small 3D printing business in Saudi Arabia for roughly 3,000–3,500 SAR: a Bambu Lab A1 mini Combo (currently 2,699 SAR) plus 5–10 spools of PLA filament at 59 SAR per 1 kg. Filament costs about 0.06 SAR per gram (59 SAR ÷ 1,000 g), so a 10 g keychain uses about 0.6 SAR of material. Popular products are personalised gifts, keychains, desk items, cosplay parts, replacement parts and architecture models. Sell through Instagram, a Salla or Zid store and local events, check the current Ministry of Commerce requirements (such as Maroof or a commercial registration) before you start selling, and avoid selling licensed characters without permission.

What to print and sell

  • Personalised gifts: name plates, Saudi map decor, desk organisers and lamps. Gifts for National Day, Eid, graduations and weddings create seasonal demand.
  • Keychains: cheap to print, quick to sell and easy to personalise with names, initials or company logos. Good for bulk corporate orders.
  • Gaming accessories: controller stands and headset holders are popular with gamers.
  • Cosplay parts: helmets, masks and props. These need a larger bed, such as the A2L’s 330 x 320 x 325 mm.
  • Spare parts and functional prints: clips, brackets, knobs and replacement parts for appliances and furniture. PETG is the usual material for parts that need strength.
  • Architecture and engineering models: students and offices need scale models and prototypes, often with short deadlines.

For reference, the 3D printed gifts in our own 3D artwork shop currently sell from 10 SAR for a single keychain to 45–85 SAR for controller stands, which gives you a sense of market prices.

Prices include VAT. Add delivery (Standard 30 SAR or Fast 50 SAR; regular delivery is free on 3,000 SAR or more of printers, so the A2L and P2S setups qualify). A spare A1-series hot end (110 SAR) is a sensible extra for A1 mini and A2L owners, because a clogged nozzle on a busy week means lost orders. Budget a little for packaging, a digital scale and a small stock of keyrings and magnets.

Worked examples with PLA at 0.059 SAR/g:

  • A 10 g keychain: about 0.59 SAR of filament.
  • A 150 g controller stand: about 8.85 SAR of filament.
  • A 400 g cosplay part: about 23.60 SAR of filament.

Your slicer shows the exact grams for each model. Add a margin for failed prints and supports (10–20% is a practical starting assumption), and remember that multi-color prints waste extra filament when the printer purges between colors. Electricity is small: the A1 mini is rated at 150 W maximum, so even a 5-hour print uses at most 0.75 kWh. The real costs are your time (design, removing supports, packing, messaging customers) and delivery. Price for those, not just for plastic.

Where to sell

  • Instagram and TikTok: short videos of a print in progress sell better than photos. Post finished products, behind-the-scenes clips and customer pick-ups.
  • Your own online store: Saudi platforms such as Salla and Zid let you open a store with local payment methods and shipping integrations.
  • Local events and bazaars: seasonal markets, university events and National Day activities are good for impulse buys like keychains.
  • Companies and schools: bulk logo keychains and giveaways are steady orders once you have samples to show.

Licensing and legal notes

Before you start selling, check the current requirements with the Ministry of Commerce and related official platforms, including whether you need Maroof registration, a commercial registration (CR) or another type of permit for your situation. Requirements can change, so rely on the official sources rather than social media advice. If your sales grow, also check VAT registration rules with ZATCA.

Intellectual property

Characters from anime, films, games and sports clubs are protected by their owners. Selling prints of licensed characters or logos without permission can lead to takedowns or legal claims. Also check the license on every downloaded model: many free files on model sites are for personal, non-commercial use only. The safest approach is to sell your own designs, files you have bought with a commercial license, or customers’ own logos with their permission.

Grow step by step

Start with one printer and a small range of 5–10 products, track which ones sell, then add a second printer or a bigger one. If a customer asks for something you cannot print yet, such as a very large part, you can send it through the 3D printing service or get a price from the instant quote tool.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a 3D printing business in Saudi Arabia?

About 3,000–3,500 SAR for a starter setup: a Bambu Lab A1 mini Combo at 2,699 SAR plus 5–10 spools of PLA at 59 SAR each. A larger A2L Combo setup is about 4,800 SAR with 10 spools.

How much does one 3D print cost to make?

With PLA at 59 SAR per kg, filament costs about 0.059 SAR per gram: roughly 0.59 SAR for a 10 g keychain and 8.85 SAR for a 150 g stand, before failed prints, packaging, time and delivery.

Do I need a licence to sell 3D printed products in Saudi Arabia?

Check with the Ministry of Commerce and its official platforms, including Maroof and commercial registration options, because the right requirement depends on how and where you sell and the rules can change.

Can I sell 3D printed anime or game characters?

Not without permission from the rights holder. Sell original designs, commercially licensed files or customers’ own designs instead.
