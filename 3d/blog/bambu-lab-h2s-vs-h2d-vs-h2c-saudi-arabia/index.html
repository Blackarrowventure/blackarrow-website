Bambu Lab H2S vs H2D vs H2C: Which H2 Printer to Buy in Saudi Arabia

The H2 series is Bambu Lab's large, enclosed, high-temperature range for engineering materials and serious production. All three share the same frame, 350 °C hot ends and a heated chamber. The difference is the toolhead: one nozzle, two nozzles, or a hotend changer.

Quick answer

  • H2S (9,999 SAR with AMS 2 Pro): one nozzle and the biggest build volume of the three. The best value if you print large parts in one or a few materials.
  • H2D (13,999 SAR with AMS 2 Pro): two nozzles, so you can print a part in one material and its supports in another, or two colors with less waste.
  • H2C (15,990 SAR): two nozzles, and the right side changes between several hotends stored in the printer. Built for many-color work with far less filament wasted on purging.

What all three share

  • Engineering materials. 350 °C hardened-steel hot ends, a 120 °C bed and an actively heated chamber up to 65 °C let them print ABS, ASA, PC, nylon (PA), PPS and carbon- or glass-fiber composites, as well as PLA, PETG and TPU.
  • Filtration. The H2S and H2D use a G3 pre-filter, an H12 HEPA filter and activated carbon, useful when printing ABS and ASA indoors.
  • Up to 24 filaments. They connect up to 4 AMS 2 Pro and 8 AMS HT units. The first-generation AMS also works (without drying); the AMS lite is not supported.
  • Size. About 49 x 51 x 63 cm and 30 kg or more: plan a sturdy bench, not a desk shelf.

H2S: one nozzle, most space

The H2S has the largest build volume in the series (340 x 320 x 340 mm) and the lowest price. Choose it for big functional parts, enclosures, jigs and fixtures, or large prints in one material. It supports the 10 W laser and blade cutting through Bambu Lab's upgrade kit. It cannot be upgraded into an H2D later, so choose the toolhead you need now.

H2D: two nozzles

With two identical nozzles, the H2D can print a part and its support in different materials (for example PLA with a dedicated support filament, which peels off cleanly), or two colors with fewer swaps. It is the right choice for engineering parts with complex supports and for print businesses that mix materials.

H2C: the hotend changer

The H2C keeps the dual-nozzle layout, and the right side swaps between induction-heated hotends stored in the printer. Each color can have its own hotend, so a many-color print wastes much less filament than a single-nozzle printer flushing between colors. It ships with 8 hotends and supports blade cutting, with a laser upgrade kit available.

Do you need an H2 at all?

If your parts fit in 256 mm and you mainly print PLA, PETG, ABS and ASA, a smaller enclosed printer costs far less: the P1S (3,499 SAR), the P2S Combo (6,299 SAR), or the dual-nozzle X2D Combo (7,899 SAR) with a 256 x 256 x 260 mm volume and a heated chamber. See P1S vs P2S. For tool changing with up to 9 tools, see the Bambu Lab H3C.

FAQ

What is the difference between the H2S and H2D?

The H2S has one nozzle and a slightly bigger build volume; the H2D has two nozzles for printing two materials or colors with less waste. Both have 350 °C hot ends, a 120 °C bed and a heated chamber.

Can the H2S be upgraded to an H2D?

No. Bambu Lab recommends choosing the model you need when you order.

Do the H2 printers work with the AMS lite?

No. They work with the AMS 2 Pro, the AMS HT and the first-generation AMS. See AMS lite vs AMS 2 Pro vs AMS HT.
