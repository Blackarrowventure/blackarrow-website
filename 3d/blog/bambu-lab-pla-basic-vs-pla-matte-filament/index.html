Bambu Lab PLA Basic vs PLA Matte Filament: Settings, Colours, Spool or Refill

Bambu Lab makes its own PLA for its printers and the AMS. PLA Basic is the everyday all-rounder; PLA Matte trades a little strength for a soft, non-glossy finish that hides layer lines. Here is how they differ, the settings Bambu Lab recommends, what the RFID tag does, and whether to buy a spool or a refill.

Quick answer

Pick Bambu Lab PLA Basic for most prints: toys, gifts, household parts and anything you want bright and a little tougher. Pick Bambu Lab PLA Matte for display pieces, decor, figures and architectural models, where a smooth non-glossy surface with fewer visible layer lines matters more than strength. Both are 1.75 mm, 1 kg, print with the same settings, and carry an RFID tag that the AMS reads to set everything up for you. Both are now available at Black Arrow 3D for 140 SAR per 1 kg, in limited stock.

The short version: PLA Basic is the stronger of the two, especially between layers (the Z values), so it is the better choice for clips, brackets and anything that takes a load. PLA Matte is about looks.

Bambu Lab printed its own test pieces at 220 °C on a 35 °C bed at 200 mm/s, a sensible starting point if you are setting up a profile by hand. If the first layer will not stick, clean the plate first; our first-layer guide covers the rest.

RFID and the AMS

Every Bambu Lab spool has an RFID tag with the print settings stored on it. Load it into any AMS (AMS lite, AMS 2 Pro or AMS HT) and the printer reads the material, colour and settings automatically, so the right profile is used and the slicer shows the right colour. Bambu Lab lists both filaments as All AMS Series Compatible. That is the main reason to buy Bambu filament for a Bambu printer: multi-colour prints just work without setting up each slot. If you are new to multi-colour, read multi-colour 3D printing explained.

Filament with spool or refill?

Bambu Lab sells each colour two ways:

  • Filament with spool: the filament comes wound on Bambu Lab's own spool, ready to load. The spool is ABS, rated to 70 °C, 200 mm across and 67 mm high.
  • Refill: the same filament without a spool. Bambu Lab's own note: the refill needs to be used with a spool. You fit it onto an empty Bambu Lab spool you already have, so there is less plastic to throw away each time.

If this is your first Bambu Lab filament, buy it with a spool. Once you have an empty spool, a refill is the tidier way to buy the next kilo. On our store you choose With spool or Refill under the colour on the PLA Basic and PLA Matte pages; refills are listed but not available yet.

Saudi heat and humidity

Both filaments start to soften in the mid-50s °C (heat deflection 54 °C for PLA Basic, 52 °C for PLA Matte at 1.8 MPa). That is fine indoors, but a car in a Saudi summer gets far hotter, so do not leave PLA prints on a dashboard. For car and outdoor parts use PETG; see PLA vs PETG vs ABS.

Bambu Lab recommends printing and storing these filaments below 20% relative humidity, sealed with desiccant. In Jeddah, Dammam and Khobar the air is far wetter than that, so keep opened spools in the AMS with fresh desiccant or in a sealed box. If a spool has taken on moisture, Bambu Lab's drying setting is 50 °C for 8 hours in a forced-air drying oven, kept away from the heater. Bambu Lab warns against using a microwave or kitchen oven, which can damage the filament. Our heat and humidity guide has more on storage.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Bambu PLA Basic and PLA Matte?

The finish and the strength. PLA Matte has a non-glossy surface that hides layer lines and suits decor and display pieces. PLA Basic is stronger, with higher tensile and bending strength in Bambu Lab's data sheets, and suits everyday and functional prints. Both use the same print settings.

Does Bambu Lab filament work in the AMS lite?

Yes. Bambu Lab lists PLA Basic and PLA Matte as compatible with all AMS series, including the AMS lite on the A1 and A1 mini. The AMS reads the RFID tag and sets the filament type and colour for you.

Can I use a refill without a spool?

No. Bambu Lab says the refill needs to be used with a spool. Buy your first kilo with a spool, keep the empty spool, and fit refills onto it after that.

What temperature should I print Bambu PLA at?

Bambu Lab recommends a nozzle temperature of 190–230 °C and a bed temperature of 35–45 °C, with the cooling fan on. On a Bambu Lab printer with an AMS the RFID tag sets this automatically.

Do Bambu Lab filaments work on other printers?

They are standard 1.75 mm PLA, so they print on other FDM printers that take 1.75 mm filament; use the settings above. Only the RFID auto-setup needs a Bambu Lab AMS.
