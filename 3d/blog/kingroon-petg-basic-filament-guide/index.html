KINGROON PETG Basic Filament Guide: When to Use PETG and How to Print It

PETG is the filament to reach for when a print has to survive heat, sunlight, water or a few knocks. Here is when KINGROON PETG Basic is the right choice over PLA, how to print it cleanly, and how to stop the stringing PETG is known for.

Quick answer

Use KINGROON PETG Basic for parts that go in a car, outdoors, in a kitchen or bathroom, or anywhere warm: phone and dashcam mounts, plant pots, hooks, brackets and covers. PETG handles heat better than PLA, bends before it snaps and shrugs off water. It prints on the same open printers as PLA, including the Bambu Lab A1 and A1 mini, with a little more care for stringing and the first layer.

Settings that work

  • Temperature: follow the range on your spool label. For Bambu Lab printers, start with the Generic PETG profile and adjust in 5 °C steps: hotter if layers split, cooler if it strings.
  • Bed: 70–80 °C. A textured PEI plate is ideal. On a smooth PEI plate, PETG can stick too well; use a thin layer of glue stick as a release layer.
  • First layer: do not squash it as hard as PLA. A slightly higher first layer avoids the nozzle dragging through the plastic.
  • Cooling: lower than PLA, around 30–50% part fan. Too much cooling weakens the layer bond.
  • Speed: PETG prefers a little slower than PLA. If the profile is very fast and walls look rough, drop the outer wall speed.

How to stop PETG stringing

  1. Dry the spool. Wet PETG strings far more. KINGROON recommends 55 °C for 8 hours if it has absorbed moisture. A filament dryer or a printer box with drying works best.
  2. Lower the temperature by 5 °C at a time.
  3. Check retraction is on. The default Bambu profiles already handle this well.
  4. Clean the nozzle. PETG likes to collect a blob on the nozzle; wipe it while the nozzle is hot, carefully, with a brass brush.

Saudi conditions

In a parked car in summer, a PLA mount can droop within a day. PETG holds its shape much better, which makes it the practical choice for phone holders, cable clips and dashcam mounts. For parts that sit in direct sun for months, choose a darker or opaque colour; transparent colours show sun damage first. In humid cities, keep PETG sealed with silica gel even more strictly than PLA.

Frequently asked questions

Can the Bambu Lab A1 mini print PETG?

Yes. The A1 mini bed reaches 80 °C and the hot end 300 °C, which is enough for PETG. Use the Generic PETG profile and a textured plate.

Is PETG stronger than PLA?

PETG is tougher: it bends and absorbs knocks where PLA snaps. PLA is stiffer. For clips, hinges and mounts PETG lasts longer.

Does PETG need an enclosed printer?

No. Unlike ABS and ASA, PETG prints well on open printers such as the A1 and A1 mini.

Can I put PETG and PLA in the AMS lite together?

Yes, you can load both, but print one material per part. PETG and PLA do not bond well to each other, which makes PLA a handy easy-to-remove support material under PETG.
