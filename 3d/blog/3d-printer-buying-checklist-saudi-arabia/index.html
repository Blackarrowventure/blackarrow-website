12-Point Checklist Before Buying a 3D Printer in Saudi Arabia

Buying your first 3D printer is easier with a checklist. These 12 points cover the questions that matter most in Saudi Arabia, from summer heat and 220 V power to delivery and warranty.

Quick answer

Before buying your first 3D printer in Saudi Arabia, check these 12 points: (1) total budget including filament, (2) build size, (3) enclosed or open frame for heat and materials, (4) multi-color needs, (5) noise, (6) app and software, (7) support in Arabic and English, (8) 220–230 V power and plug, (9) delivery time to your city, (10) warranty handling, (11) filament types and availability, and (12) learning resources. For most beginners, a printer with automatic calibration, a 180–256 mm build volume and PLA filament is the right start, for example the Bambu Lab A1 mini Combo at 2,699 SAR (currently in stock).

The 12-point checklist

1. Budget, including filament

Do not spend your whole budget on the printer. Plan for 3–5 spools of filament at 65–75 SAR each (about 195–375 SAR), delivery (30 SAR Standard or 50 SAR Fast, free on every printer order) and, later, a spare nozzle or hot end. If you plan to pay with Tabby or Tamara, add the 100 SAR pay-later service fee on printer orders.

2. Build size

Build volume decides the largest single piece you can print. As a guide, using current catalog sizes:

3. Enclosed or open frame (heat matters in Saudi Arabia)

Open-frame printers are simpler and print PLA, PETG and TPU very well. Enclosed printers keep heat in, which materials like ABS and ASA need to avoid warping and cracking. In a hot Saudi summer, keep any printer in an air-conditioned room, away from direct sun and AC drafts. For PLA in an enclosed printer, opening the door or lid in a warm room helps avoid heat-related clogs. Enclosed options currently in stock include the Bambu Lab P2S Combo (6,299 SAR) and the Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 (3,050 SAR).

4. Multi-color printing

If you want colorful figures, logos or name plates, choose a printer that supports a multi-color system: Bambu Lab’s AMS lite or AMS 2 Pro, Elegoo’s CANVAS (up to 4 filaments) or the Snapmaker U1 with 4 independent toolheads. Multi-color prints take longer and use extra filament for purging between colors. Read multi-color 3D printing explained.

5. Noise

All 3D printers make noise from fans and motors, and prints can run for many hours. If the printer will sit in a bedroom or a shared office, plan a spot where it can run overnight without disturbing anyone. Enclosed printers tend to contain some of the sound.

6. App and software

Check how you will send files: Wi-Fi with a phone or desktop app, USB, or a memory card. The A1 mini, for example, supports Wi-Fi and Micro SD; the P2S works with Bambu Handy and Bambu Studio. Check the interface languages before you buy if that matters to you, and remember that many tutorials and slicer menus are in English.

7. Support

You will almost certainly have questions in the first weeks. Buy from a seller who answers in your language. Black Arrow 3D supports customers in Arabic and English on WhatsApp +966 56 022 4715 and at sales@blackarrowksa.com.

8. Power: 220–230 V and plug type

Saudi homes use 220–230 V. Check the printer’s input rating on the spec sheet: the A1 mini, for example, is rated 100–240 VAC, 50/60 Hz, 150 W max. Also check that the plug fits your sockets (many newer Saudi buildings use UK-style three-pin sockets) or plan a good-quality adapter, and use a surge-protected power strip.

9. Delivery time

From Black Arrow 3D in Dammam, in-stock items ordered before 4 pm (Saudi time), Sunday to Thursday, ship the same day. Standard delivery takes 4–5 business days and Fast delivery 2–3 business days to anywhere in Saudi Arabia. Pre-order items, such as the Bambu Lab H2C currently, ship when stock arrives.

10. Warranty

Printers carry the manufacturer’s warranty for defects. Ask who helps you with the claim and how the process works. Black Arrow 3D helps customers with warranty claims. Also read the return and exchange policy before ordering.

11. Filament types and availability

Start with PLA or PLA+: easy to print, low warping, no enclosure needed. Move to PETG for stronger, more heat-tolerant parts, and TPU for flexible items. Check the printer’s supported materials: the A2L, for example, is best with PLA, PETG and TPU, with ABS and ASA not recommended. Current local prices: KINGROON PLA 65 SAR, KINGROON PETG 65 SAR, PLA+ 75 SAR per 1 kg. Humid coastal cities like Dammam and Jeddah make dry storage important.

12. Learning resources

Choose a popular printer with a big community, so tutorials, ready-made profiles and model libraries are easy to find. Start with our guides: A1 mini setup, first-layer adhesion and printer maintenance.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best first 3D printer to buy in Saudi Arabia?

For most beginners, a printer with automatic calibration and a mid-size bed. The Bambu Lab A1 mini Combo (2,699 SAR, 180 mm cube) suits small items, and the A2L Combo (4,200 SAR, 330 x 320 x 325 mm) suits larger prints. See best 3D printer for beginners.

Do I need an enclosed 3D printer in Saudi Arabia’s heat?

Not for PLA, PETG or TPU, as long as the printer is in an air-conditioned room. You need an enclosed printer mainly for ABS and ASA.

How much should I budget for my first 3D printer?

About 3,000 SAR for a beginner setup: an A1 mini Combo at 2,699 SAR plus 3–5 spools of filament at 65–75 SAR each.

Will a 3D printer work on Saudi 220 V power?

Most modern printers accept a wide input range; the A1 mini is rated 100–240 VAC. Check the rating for your model and whether the plug fits your sockets.
