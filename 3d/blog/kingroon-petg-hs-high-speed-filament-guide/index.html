KINGROON PETG HS High-Speed Filament: Settings, Strength and Who It Suits

KINGROON PETG HS is PETG made for fast printers. KINGROON says it prints up to twice as fast as standard PETG by default, with less stringing and a matte finish. Here are the official settings, how it compares with PLA, and where it earns its place in Saudi heat.

Quick answer

Choose KINGROON PETG HS when you want PETG's toughness and heat resistance but print on a fast machine such as a Bambu Lab A1, P2S or a Snapmaker U1. KINGROON says its default print speed is up to twice as fast as standard PETG, with less oozing, stringing and clumping, and a matte finish that stays even when the speed changes. It prints at 230–260 °C on a 65–75 °C bed. At Black Arrow 3D it costs 65 SAR per 1 kg in Silver, Brown, Green, Orange and Yellow.

What HS means

HS stands for high speed. Ordinary PETG is happiest printed a little slower than PLA: push it fast and it strings, blobs and shows glossy bands wherever the speed changes. KINGROON reformulated PETG HS to flow and cool fast enough for modern high-speed printers, rating it for print speeds under 300 mm/s. The matte surface is part of the same idea: KINGROON says it removes the uneven gloss shifts you get with standard PETG during speed changes, so walls look uniform.

KINGROON printed its own test pieces at 255 °C on a 70 °C bed, which is a good starting point. On a Bambu Lab printer, start from a Generic PETG profile in Bambu Studio and set the nozzle and bed temperatures above. Glue on a smooth PEI plate matters with PETG: it acts as a release layer as well as an adhesive, so the part does not bond too hard to the plate.

The pattern is the usual PLA vs PETG one. PLA is stiffer and bonds layers more strongly. PETG HS takes more heat before it softens and absorbs knocks better, so it is the one for parts that get warm or get handled roughly.

Saudi heat

PETG HS starts to soften at about 62–70 °C, against about 54–57 °C for PLA. That makes it the safer choice for things that sit near a window, in a hot store room or outside in the shade, and for cable clips, brackets and hooks. A car parked in the summer sun is harder: a dashboard can get hotter than both, so keep printed parts out of direct sun on the dash, or choose a darker, thicker design and test it first.

Drying and storage

PETG picks up moisture from the air, and wet PETG strings and pops. KINGROON recommends drying it at 65 °C for 8 hours in a forced-air oven or filament dryer before printing, and keeping it below 20% relative humidity, sealed with desiccant. Do not use a kitchen oven or microwave: KINGROON warns they can damage the filament. In Jeddah, Dammam and Khobar, store opened spools in a sealed box with silica gel. FAQ

Is KINGROON PETG HS faster than normal PETG?

KINGROON says its default print speed is up to twice as fast as standard PETG, rated for print speeds under 300 mm/s, with less oozing, stringing and clumping at speed.

What temperature should I print KINGROON PETG HS at?

KINGROON recommends a nozzle temperature of 230–260 °C and a bed temperature of 65–75 °C, on a smooth or textured PEI plate with glue, with the part fan at 0–60%.

Is PETG HS glossy?

No. KINGROON PETG HS has a matte finish, which also hides the gloss bands that ordinary PETG shows where the print speed changes.

Is PETG HS better than PLA?

For heat and impact, yes: in KINGROON's data it softens at a higher temperature and absorbs more impact. PLA is stiffer, bonds layers more strongly and is easier to print, so it is still the better choice for decor, gifts and display pieces.

Does it work on Bambu Lab printers?

Yes, it is standard 1.75 mm filament. Start from a Generic PETG profile and set KINGROON's temperatures. There is no RFID tag, so set the filament type and colour in the slicer yourself.
