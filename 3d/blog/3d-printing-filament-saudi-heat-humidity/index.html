3D Printing in Saudi Heat and Humidity: Filament, Storage, Placement

Humid coastal air soaks filament, and a Saudi summer can soften a finished print in a parked car. Here is how to store, dry and choose filament for the climate you actually print in.

Quick answer: The Saudi climate affects 3D printing in two ways. On the humid coast (Jeddah, Dammam, Khobar, Jubail) filament absorbs moisture from the air, which causes stringing, bubbles and weak prints, so keep spools sealed with desiccant and dry them before printing (PLA at about 45–55 °C, PETG at about 55–65 °C). Everywhere in the Kingdom, summer heat softens printed parts: PLA starts to soften at roughly 55–60 °C, so anything that will live in a car or on a sunny balcony should be printed in PETG (about 75–80 °C) or, better, ASA or ABS (about 100 °C).

Two climates, two different problems

Saudi Arabia is not one climate for a 3D printer. The coast and the interior cause different problems, and the fix depends on where you live.

RegionMain riskWhat to do
Humid coast: Jeddah, Dammam, Khobar, Jubail, YanbuFilament absorbs moisture within days of openingSealed storage with desiccant, dry before long prints, print from a dry box
Dry inland: Riyadh, Qassim, Hail, Al-KharjExtreme heat, fine dust, big day-to-night temperature swingsKeep the printer in an air-conditioned room, cover it when idle, clean rails and the build plate often
Everywhere in summerPrinted parts softening in cars, on balconies and in garagesChoose the material by where the part will live, not by what is easiest to print

Inland makers are not completely safe from moisture either: an opened spool left on a shelf for months, or carried between an air-conditioned room and a hot garage, still picks up water. It just happens more slowly.

How to store filament in a humid city

  • Seal every opened spool. Use a zip bag, vacuum bag or airtight box with a few silica gel packs. Indicator silica gel changes color when it is saturated, so you know when to dry or replace it.
  • Print from a dry box on long prints, so the spool is not exposed to room air for 10 or 20 hours.
  • Mind cardboard spools. Some filament, including the PLA+ we sell, comes on a cardboard reel. Cardboard also holds moisture, so store it sealed like any other spool.
  • Do not store spools in a car, garage or outdoor storeroom. Heat and humidity cycles there are the fastest way to ruin filament.

Signs your filament is already wet: popping or crackling sounds while printing, fine strings between parts, a rough or bubbly surface, and PLA that snaps when you bend it. Our stringing guide covers the print-side fixes.

Drying temperatures by material

A dedicated filament dryer is the most reliable option; a food dehydrator also works. Avoid a kitchen oven: its thermostat can overshoot and soften the spool. These are typical starting points; always check the label on your spool first.

MaterialDrying temperatureTypical time
PLA / PLA+45–55 °C4–6 hours
PETG55–65 °C6–8 hours
TPU50–55 °C6–8 hours
ABS / ASA60–80 °C4–6 hours

For example, the KINGROON PETG Basic listing recommends 55 °C for 8 hours if the filament has absorbed moisture. Keep PLA below its softening point, or the spool can deform and jam.

Which materials survive a car or balcony in a Saudi summer?

The number to look at is the glass transition temperature: the point where a plastic stops being rigid and starts to soften. It is well below the melting point, and parts under load, such as a phone mount clip, bend first. The figures below are approximate and vary by brand and blend.

MaterialApprox. glass transitionInside a parked car in summerOn a sunny balcony
PLA / PLA+about 55–60 °CLikely to warp or sagSoftens and fades; indoor use only
PETGabout 75–80 °CFine for most cabin parts; risky on a dashboard in direct sunAcceptable in shade; weakens slowly in strong sun
ABSabout 100–105 °CGood heat resistanceHeat is fine, but UV makes it brittle and yellow over time
ASAabout 100 °CGood heat resistanceThe best common choice for outdoor parts

A closed car in July or August gets much hotter than the air outside, and a dashboard in direct sun hotter still. That is why a PLA phone holder that works perfectly in an office can droop after one afternoon in a mall car park. Simple rule: PLA for indoor and air-conditioned use, PETG as the minimum for car interiors and shaded outdoor spots, ASA for anything in direct sun.

ABS and ASA need an enclosed printer and good ventilation. Among the printers we currently list, the enclosed Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 (currently 3,050 SAR) and the Bambu Lab H2C (currently 15,990 SAR) list ABS and ASA among their supported materials. Open printers such as the Bambu Lab A1 mini and A2L are made for PLA, PETG and TPU.

Where to put your printer

  • Away from AC drafts. Cold air from a split unit blowing across the build plate cools one side of the print faster than the other, which causes warping, corners lifting and layer splits. Do not place the printer directly under or in front of an AC vent.
  • In an air-conditioned room, not a garage. Very hot rooms make printing harder: PLA can soften inside the extruder and cause clogs (often called heat creep), and parts cool too slowly between layers.
  • Out of direct sunlight through a window, which heats one side of the machine and fades spools.
  • Protected from dust. In Riyadh and Qassim fine dust settles on rails, fans and the build plate. Cover the printer when idle, wipe the plate with isopropyl alcohol before printing, and follow a simple maintenance routine.

Do you need an enclosure?

For PLA, no: open printers handle PLA well, and on an enclosed printer you usually open the door or lid for PLA so heat does not build up. For PETG an enclosure is optional. For ABS and ASA it is essential, together with ventilation for fumes. In Saudi homes an enclosure has two extra benefits: it shields the print from AC drafts and keeps dust out. If you mainly print decor and gifts in PLA, an open printer is fine; if you want heat-resistant car and outdoor parts, plan for an enclosed one. Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my filament has absorbed moisture?

Listen and look: popping or crackling from the nozzle, more stringing than usual, a rough or bubbly surface, and brittle PLA that snaps when bent are the typical signs. Drying the spool usually fixes all of them.

Can I leave a PLA print in my car in Saudi Arabia?

Not in summer. PLA starts to soften at roughly 55–60 °C, and a parked car in the sun gets hotter than that. Print car parts in PETG at minimum, or ASA for anything on the dashboard.

What is the best filament for outdoor use in Saudi Arabia?

ASA, because it combines a glass transition of about 100 °C with good UV resistance. PETG is a workable budget option for shaded spots. Avoid PLA outdoors.

Do I need to dry filament if I live in Riyadh?

Less often than on the coast, because the air is drier. Still store opened spools sealed, and dry any spool that has been open for weeks or shows the signs above, especially PETG and TPU.
